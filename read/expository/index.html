Why the SK order parameter has no gaps

A variational argument from contact to strict curvature

Will Cook · Expository notes · 3 November 2026

AI assistance. Will Cook directed the work. AI agents based on large language models produced and revised this manuscript.

The Sherrington–Kirkpatrick (SK) model asks us to choose signs that maximise a random sum of pair interactions. Its limiting maximum is expressed by the Parisi formula through a nondecreasing function, the order parameter. We explain why a minimising order parameter is strictly increasing, that is, why it cannot be constant on any interval below one.

Suppose the order parameter were constant on an interval. Minimality fixes the values of an auxiliary curve at the two ends of that interval and bounds its slopes there, while an analytic estimate makes the derivative of the curve strictly convex in between. The curve is then left with too little area to connect the required values. Initial and terminal gaps need separate arguments. Finally we return to the spins, where a theorem about their energies gives nearly optimal sign choices differing on any prescribed positive fraction of positions up to one half, with arbitrarily small error in that fraction.

We follow the quantitative proof of OpenAI (2026a). Full support had already been proved by a different method in Hong-Bin Chen (2026), Theorem 1.3, and the estimate used here makes the final contradiction a direct comparison of areas. These notes assume algebra and elementary calculus, and they introduce probability and cumulative mass through calculations. Deeper analytic facts are stated where the proof needs them. By the end of Section 6 the reader should be able to reconstruct how minimality and those inputs exclude each kind of gap, and to translate the result into a statement about spin configurations. Section 7 collects optional calculations and the answers to the short questions in the text. The full analytic proofs are in the cited sources.
